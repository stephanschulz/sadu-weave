Wall & cables

Loom

The warp is the cables; it runs down the wall. Bands run along the warp — vertical strips — laid out symmetrically about the centre. Every motif is a small 0/1 chart: one column per pattern pair, one row per pick. At each tie row the strands of a band gather into that row's dark cells. The warping is the program.

With diagonal moves a strand that changes column is built as a straight cable between the two tie nodes instead of down-then-along-the-row. One cell per row on a square grid is 45°; a 173 mm row pitch makes one cell per row exactly 30° from vertical; 115 mm rows with two cells per row make 60°.

lane offset lifts rightward runs above and leftward runs below the row line so they don't draw on top of each other; 0 keeps every horizontal exactly on the grid line.

Structural columns. The plain ground strands between tiles (or bands) run straight to the bottom row: each keeps this many cables in reserve — never given to a rib, never dropped to a bulb before the last row — so there is always a live vertical to tie to. A run may only land on a column that provably continues below: a structural column, or a strand with at least two cables.

Support rule. A hanging net has no bottom beam, so a rib is only a structural member if it is tensioned — between the strands whose cables arrive vertically that row (anchors) and, with edge rails, the wall. Every 90° corner must sit on an anchor, or on a rib where the sag it causes, W·a·b / (T·L), stays under the allowed sag — W is what that bundle still carries, a and b the distances to the nearest anchors. Moves that cannot be supported are shortened; a run may only land on a strand that still has cables. Corners that still fail are counted and drawn as red ✕.

twine is the over/under between neighbouring strands, in pattern-pair pitches: above 0.5 they cross each other and cross back every period. It sits on top of the chart like the twining sits on top of the weave.

Weft & ribs — cables travelling across

Every cable starts at the beam, but at a weft row some leave their strand, run horizontally along the row line, are tied at every strand they cross, and continue down with the strand where the run ends — and may go over again at a later weft row, direction alternating like a shuttle. Runs that start closer together than they are long overlap and hand over: several cables make one continuous rib across the full width, 2–3 cables thick, holding the strands at that height.

At 1 there is a rib at every pick — horizontal lines at the same pitch as the vertical ones, every strand tied at every row: a net, not a curtain of independent strands.

figure on the net

holes are the only figure that reads on an all-diagonal net; the panel shows what they cost (every hole edge is pulled open — fine near the beam, large shifts low on the wall). chevrons flip both families at every node inside the figure so the pulls cancel — balanced, but a strand zigzagging between two columns traces the very diamonds the lattice already draws, so nothing shows. density keeps every diagonal straight and the net uniform: inside the figure cables drop to their bulbs sooner (× factor), so the figure reads as thin lines and bright bulbs against thick lines — up to 14 : 1 in line weight, no extra load anywhere. < 1 inverts it. holes reflect the strands off the figure like off the rails; the panel shows what that costs: every hole edge is pulled open, and low on the wall a light node can shift by hundreds of millimetres.

Every cable is a straight diagonal. From each start column (every start every cells) two strands leave the beam, one at +θ and one at −θ, cross the other family at every node and bounce off the edge rails. No ribs, no anchor columns: at every crossing the two families' sideways pulls cancel, so the net holds itself — the rails only take the bounces. θ comes from the grid: one cell per row on a square grid is 45°; row pitch 173 mm → 30°; 115 mm with 2 cells per row → 60°.

One motif repeated across the whole width, each column of it shifted down by stagger rows from its neighbour. ground between ≥ 1 puts a plain strand between tiles that never moves — the anchor the ribs pull against. Without it, heavy bundles near the beam have nothing within reach and the support rule freezes them.

Borders outside → centre

Centre band — shajarah

Border bands are short-period repeats from a fixed lexicon; ground after puts that many plain warp columns (straight, untwined strands) between a band and the next. The centre is the free band: a sequence of discrete glyphs separated by ground, read like a line of text. Ground rows spread the strands evenly; glyph rows gather them.

Bulb drop

A new bulb layout changes only where cables drop and how long they are — the weave (every strand path, every rib) stays identical: the design seed fixes how many cables leave each strand at each row, the bulb seed re-decides which ones, their tail lengths, and their drift: with that probability a dropping cable first runs along the rib up to reach cells and hangs from any point of the rib (a rib is a tensioned horizontal, so the tie can sit anywhere along it) — so bulbs stop lining up under their strands and under the grid columns.

Assumptions & model

• Flat elevation: everything is one plane against the wall, no depth — except at a crossing, where one strand passes in front of the other.
• Strands are tied to one another at every tie row they pass — that is what holds a lateral run. A "loose weave" still needs these ties.
• Below its last tie a cable hangs plumb, so the bulb x equals that tie's x. Tails spread the bulb heights; the blue-noise picker spreads the x.
• The nylon core means no stretch: arc length is conserved, so the cut length is exactly path + tail.
• Tension at a tie = everything hanging below it. Lateral pull = T·sinθ; it must be resisted by the tie into the neighbouring strand, and the net per row must land in the edge restraints.
