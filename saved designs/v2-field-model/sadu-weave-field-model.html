Wall & cables

Strands (reads the top)

1000 cables side-by-side over 7 m = 7 mm pitch on a 5.5 mm cable — a solid curtain. Grouping them into strands that fork downward is what lets pattern into the upper wall, and it is what al sadu is: warp-faced bands. Set top strands = cables for no bundling.

Lattice

max angle caps how far from vertical any weave run may lean between two tie rows. snap rounds every tie to a grid so installers work from a peg-board.

Pattern bands top → bottom

Each band has a figure (a displacement field in metres — the sadu motif) and a weave: the wall is cut into weave cells (metres); alternate cells zigzag in antiphase, so every strand in a cell swings as one bundle and crosses the neighbouring bundle, then crosses back. Bundles fully swap when amp ≥ cell/2; below cell/4 they only wiggle. The top strand pitch is 175 mm — a weave cell of that size makes every strand cross its neighbour.

Bulb drop

γ<1 sheds early (pattern opens high, bulbs crowd the top) · γ=1 even over height · γ>1 holds cables down low.

Weave

A crossing is two strands swapping left-right order between one tie row and the next. Turns are direction reversals along one cable. In the Technical view crossings are the teal dots.

Coverage vs height

Watch for the belly. Splitting a strand into single cables makes it wider (20 cables = Ø26 mm bundled, 110 mm side-by-side), so coverage can climb as you fork even while cables shed. Gold is real bundled coverage, grey is cables laid flat; the dashed line is the ≈50 % below which negative space reads.

Assumptions & model

• Flat elevation: everything is one plane against the wall, no depth — except at a crossing, where one strand passes in front of the other.
• Strands are tied to one another at every lattice row they pass — that is what holds a lateral run. A "loose weave" still needs these ties.
• Below its last tie a cable hangs plumb, so the bulb x equals that tie's x. Tails spread the bulb heights; the blue-noise picker spreads the x.
• The nylon core means no stretch: arc length is conserved, so the cut length is exactly path + tail.
• Tension at a tie = everything hanging below it. Lateral pull = T·sinθ; it must be resisted by the tie into the neighbouring strand, and the net per row must land in the edge restraints.
